## Comprehensive Guide for Lagomorph Translation

## 📊 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Rabbits are highly social prey animals with a rich but **subtle communication system** that relies heavily on body language, subtle postures, and limited vocalizations. Most rabbit owners miss 90% of what their rabbit is communicating because the signals are quiet, quick, and context-dependent.

**Key Insight:** Rabbit communication is primarily **visual and olfactory** — requiring computer vision for translation. Their "vocalizations" are rare and usually indicate distress or extreme emotion.

## 🐰 RABBIT COMMUNICATION MODALITIES

### 1. Ear Positioning (Primary Signal System)

| Ear Position | Meaning | Urgency |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| **Both forward, alert** | Curious, investigating | Normal |
| **One forward, one back** | Divided attention, cautious | Normal |
| **Both back, relaxed** | Content, listening behind | Normal |
| **Both back, tense** | Fearful, ready to flee | Elevated |
| **Flattened against back** | Extreme fear, submission | High |
| **Rapidly twitching/scanning** | High alert, scanning for threats | Elevated |

**Lop vs. Upright Ears:**
- Lop rabbits (floppy ears) communicate differently — harder to read
- Must rely more on body posture and behavior
- Ear rotation is still visible at the base

### 2. Body Posture

| Posture | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Relaxed flop (side lying)** | Ultimate trust, contentment |
| **Loaf position (paws tucked)** | Comfortable, resting |
| **Sitting upright** | Alert, curious |
| **Tense crouch** | Fearful, ready to flee |
| **Stretched out front** | Relaxed but attentive |
| **Rolled on back** | Extreme trust (rare) |
| **Thumping hind leg** | Danger alarm (to other rabbits) |

### 3. Tail Signals

| Tail Position | Meaning |
|---------------|---------|
| **Up and visible** | Alert, excited |
| **Down and relaxed** | Normal, content |
| **Tucked under** | Fearful, submissive |
| **Rapid tail wag** | Defiance, excitement (context-dependent) |
| **Tail up with urination** | Territory marking (spraying) |

### 4. Nose & Whiskers

| Signal | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Rapid nose twitching** | Alert, investigating |
| **Slow nose movements** | Relaxed, content |
| **Frozen nose** | Fear freeze response |
| **Whiskers forward** | Curious, investigating |
| **Whiskers back** | Fearful, defensive |

### 5. Eye Signals

| Signal | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Wide eyes** | Alert, fearful |
| **Half-closed, relaxed** | Content, sleepy |
| **Staring fixed** | Fear freeze or intense focus |
| **Third eyelid showing** | Stress, illness |

### 6. Vocalizations (Rare but Important)

| Sound | Meaning | Context |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Grunting/Growling** | Anger, territorial warning | "Back off!" |
| **Thumping** | Danger alarm | Warning other rabbits |
| **Screaming** | Extreme fear/pain | **Emergency** |
| **Tooth purring** | Contentment | Happy rabbit |
| **Soft honking/oinking** | Affection, mating | "I like you" |
| **Squeaking** | Fear, pain, protest | Needs attention |
| **Hissing** | Aggression, fear | Rare in healthy rabbits |

**Critical Note:** Rabbits are nearly silent animals. Vocalizations usually indicate distress and should be taken seriously.

### 7. Behavioral Signals

**Binkies (Joy Jumps):**
- Mid-air twists, kicks, jumps
- Clear sign of happiness and energy
- "I'm so happy I can't contain myself!"

**Zoomies:**
- Fast running in circles or patterns
- Playful energy release
- Sign of comfort in environment

**Periscoping:**
- Standing on hind legs to see better
- Curiosity, investigating
- Can indicate alertness

**Chinning:**
- Rubbing chin on objects (scent glands)
- Territory marking
- "This is mine"

**Circling:**
- Running circles around feet/objects
- Mating behavior (even neutered rabbits)
- Can indicate affection

**Nipping:**
- Gentle nip — attention seeking, grooming request
- Hard nip — warning, boundary setting
- Repeated nipping — "Do what I want!"

**Licking/Grooming:**
- Social bonding
- Affection toward humans
- Mimics rabbit-rabbit grooming

**Flopping:**
- Sudden drop to side
- Ultimate trust signal
- "I feel completely safe here"

## 🧠 RABBIT PSYCHOLOGY

### Prey Animal Mentality

**Core Truth:** Everything in rabbit psychology is filtered through "I am prey."

**Communication Implications:**
- Signals are subtle (don't attract predator attention)
- Fear escalates quickly
- Trust takes time to build
- Comfort = survival

### Social Structure

**Bonded Pairs:**
- Rabbits form intense pair bonds
- Communicate constantly through subtle signals
- Separation causes distress
- Bonded rabbits groom each other, sleep together, mirror behaviors

**Hierarchy:**
- Establish dominance through subtle posturing
- Mounting (even same-sex pairs)
- Chin marking contests
- One rabbit typically grooms the other more

### Human-Rabbit Bond

**Trust Building Signals:**
- Approaching voluntarily
- Flopping near you
- Grooming you (licking)
- Eating in your presence
- Allowing handling without struggle

**Fear Signals:**
- Running away
- Thumping
- Freezing
- Flattened ears
- Avoidance

## 🤖 APP IMPLEMENTATION

### Detection Targets

**High Priority (Easily Detectable):**
- Ear position and movement
- Body posture (flop, loaf, crouch)
- Thumping behavior
- Binkies and zoomies
- Nose twitching rate

**Medium Priority:**
- Tail position
- Eye state
- Chinning behavior
- Periscoping

**Low Priority (Requires Context):**
- Vocalizations (audio analysis)
- Grooming behavior
- Subtle tension indicators

### Visual Recognition Challenges

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| Rabbits are low to ground | Angle detection, multiple camera positions |
| Fast movements (binkies) | High frame rate capture |
| Fur color variations | Training on diverse coat colors |
| Breed size differences | Scale normalization |
| Lop ear communication | Focus on base rotation, not ear tips |

### Health Alerts

| Signal | Possible Issue | Action |
|--------|----------------|--------|
| **Tooth grinding (loud)** | Pain (GI stasis, dental) | Vet immediately |
| **Not eating + hiding** | GI stasis | **Emergency** |
| **Labored breathing** | Respiratory issue | Vet immediately |
| **Head tilt** | Ear infection, neurological | Vet within 24h |
| **Wet chin/chest** | Dental issues, drooling | Vet within 48h |
| **Not producing feces** | GI stasis | **Emergency** |
| **Pressing head against wall** | Neurological | **Emergency** |

## 🏥 RABBIT-SPECIFIC HEALTH NOTES

### Critical: GI Stasis

Rabbits cannot vomit. GI stasis (gut slowdown) is life-threatening.

**Communication Signs:**
- Hiding
- Not eating
- Not drinking
- Small/no feces
- Pressing belly to floor
- Teeth grinding (pain)

**App Should:** Flag these combinations as emergency

### Dental Disease

Rabbits have continuously growing teeth.

**Signs:**
- Selective eating
- Drooling
- Weight loss
- Wet chin

## 🎯 KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. **Silence is Golden** — Vocalizations = distress
2. **Body Language is Everything** — Focus on posture, ears, behavior
3. **Trust Signals** — Flopping, grooming, eating near you
4. **Emergency Recognition** — App should flag GI stasis signs
5. **Bond Indicators** — Binkies, zoomies, voluntary approach
6. **Subtle is Normal** — Rabbit communication is inherently quiet
